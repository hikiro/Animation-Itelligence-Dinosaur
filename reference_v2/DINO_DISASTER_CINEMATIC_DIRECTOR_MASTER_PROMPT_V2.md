## DINO//DISASTER · Episode 01: WRONG EXIT
### Story-first cinematic direction, animation, audiovisual production, and QC

> **Instruction to the execution agent:** Produce film-quality motion that is grounded in actual rendered evidence, not claims of quality. This is a production specification for code-enabled animation tools (Remotion/Canvas/2.5D or comparable). The first deliverable is a nine-second shot benchmark, approved on visual and sound grounds **before** building the 80-second episode. Do not simply generate a slideshow, animate flat characters sliding horizontally, or use camera movement to conceal incomplete character action. If you cannot run a particular stage, accurately disclose what you did and did not inspect.

## A. Role, project lock and approvals

Act as film director, director of photography, storyboard/layout artist, character animation lead, editor, sound designer, re-recording mixer and QC supervisor. Make your departments cooperate. Do not assign a single universal easing curve to every person, prop, and camera element.

**User-approved production strategy:** FIRST execute and validate the **9-second portal-exit benchmark**; use its proven rig/portal/camera/audio architecture for the remaining 80-second film. The story concept, Japanese-inspired rough textured paper-cut style, 9:16 1080x1920 format, 30fps target and THE CURIOSITY ARCHIVE identity are provisionally locked. Final duration of narrative film approximately 80 seconds (2400 frames at 30fps). Original artwork and original/licensed audio only; no copyrighted song sampling.

**Non-negotiable order of priority:**
1. Does the viewer see the story's claimed action happen?
2. Is motivation and reaction causally coherent?
3. Are geography, occlusion and object ownership continuous?
4. Are character anatomy, mass, contacts and motion convincing within this stylized world?
5. Do camera/edit/sound guide attention and emotion?
6. Is everything technically clean and artistically cohesive?

A technical success is not artistic approval. Do not make success assertions without evidence from the rendered timeline.

## B. Core story and motivations

**Logline:** A curious human emerges through a portal into a prehistoric forest, catches a dinosaur egg before it falls, is chased by the mother who mistakes him for a thief, and reunites the hatchling with her. After he departs through the portal, the affectionate baby follows him into his apartment, forcing the mother to pursue them through the same doorway.

**Arc:** Curiosity -> wonder -> compassion -> misinterpreted threat -> panic -> discovery -> tenderness -> humorous disaster.

**Main characters and immediate wants:**
- **Human:** Explore safely; save a falling egg; protect it as danger closes; reunite it with its mother; return home. His panic is initially at odds with his compassion, creating comedy.
- **Mother dinosaur:** Locate/defend her egg. She sees a stranger holding it, interprets him as a threat, later recognizes her baby and calms. She is protective, never an arbitrary villain.
- **Baby:** Find affection; respond to both its mother and its rescuer. After bonding with human in the hatch/reunion, it intentionally follows him.
- **Portal:** Stable entry/exit linking the two locations, physically crossable by characters. It remains active throughout the prehistoric scenes, then stretches when mother attempts a too-large crossing.

**Fixed world map (continuity bible):** Portal WEST = screen LEFT, egg nest EAST = screen RIGHT, mother approaches from even farther EAST. Human exits portal, travels RIGHT to egg; when threatened, he and mother run LEFT toward the portal. After the reunion, human continues LEFT into portal; baby follows; mother delayed by inspecting hatch remains, looks up, and lunges. In apartment the other side of the portal has a consistent threshold and orientation. Use world coordinates distinct from camera coordinates. Maintain this map shot-to-shot; show an explicit neutral reorientation if an axis change is dramatically necessary.

**Cause/effect grammar:** For every beat write a seven-link trace: STIMULUS -> PERCEPTION -> INTERNAL DECISION -> ANTICIPATION -> ACTION -> CONTACT/CONSEQUENCE -> RECOVERY / next motivation. If any link necessary for understanding is invisible or missing, fix blocking/timing before polish.

## C. Art and animation identity

**Visual style:** Original Japanese-inspired handmade washi-paper/scissor-cut theatre, not a generic flat vector slideshow: paper grain and fiber in stable material coordinates; slight uneven cut outlines; offset ink accents; tactile overlapping layered body cutouts; contact shadows between layers; animated depth from foreground, action-plane and distant foliage. Characters remain graphic, delightful and recognizable from silhouette. The world has one consistent set of color/lighting/material rules, not one random style per shot.

**Character rig controls:** Human head/eyes/mouth, neck, ribcage/torso, pelvis, shoulders, upper/lower arms, gripping hands, upper/lower legs, ankles and feet. Dinosaur head/eyes/jaw, neck, torso, hips, forelimbs, thighs/shins/feet, tail multi-segment with overlap. Baby proportions and miniature mechanics are consistent with mother but not scaled-only copies. Rig pivot locations and attachments must be inspectable.

**Body performance rules:**
- Gaze points at the event *before* head/torso respond except involuntary startle.
- Force propagates through the whole character (e.g., jump or roar recoil affects feet, hips, back and arms), following the Gogan whole-body response principle.
- Foot planted in world coordinates during stance; center of mass moves over support foot; swings and landings clearly distinguished.
- Dinosaur mass: delayed acceleration/braking, pelvis load, head/tail lag, step-induced foliage response. Cartoon exaggeration permitted, but not weightless motion.
- Egg stays attached at known grip points while carried; transfer to baby and shell is explicit.
- Contact moments visibly happen on screen: stepping through portal, catching egg, branch break, hatchling extraction, setting baby down, mother-baby nuzzle and snout threshold crossing.
- Animate primary poses, then breakdowns/spacing, then overlap and secondary environment motion. Avoid keyframe pop/jitter, abrupt unblended gait change, unexplained teleport, global linear slide or cyclic limb movement divorced from root motion.

## D. Cinema grammar — mandatory before animating shots

For EACH planned shot, supply a shot record with: shot ID; world location; narrative intent; whose POV/emotional alignment; shot size (establishing/wide/medium/close/insert); virtual camera angle/height; subject screen location and look room; axis side and screen direction; lens/perspective metaphor if useful; foreground/middleground/background; focus/eye-trace target; camera motion start/end/velocity (or deliberate locked-off); entry composition; exit composition; exact action; first/last frame match; visual lighting beat; cue/ambience/music priority; and test proving the action.

### D1. Framing and gaze

- Use a wide or medium-wide **master** early to establish portal, ground, nest corridor and relative character scale; return to a geography-restoring view when needed.
- Show both participants during interactions if that best communicates cause/effect; inserts can highlight egg or foot but cannot replace the visible physical event.
- Show what a character looks at, and then their reaction; maintain eyeline vectors for human-to-mother, mother-to-egg and human-to-baby.
- Use negative space and clear silhouette. At each moment one focal event dominates via contrast, motion, scale, sharpness, gaze or framing. Verify at phone size, in grayscale, and muted.
- Use eye-line height for empathy with human, lower viewpoint for mother's intimidating scale, baby-level view for tenderness; avoid overusing Dutch tilts or extreme camera angles.

### D2. Camera movement

- Name a **narrative reason** for each camera movement. A camera can reveal space, follow the actor, emphasize scale, follow the egg's path, isolate a reaction, or increase intimacy. If there is no reason, hold the camera.
- Pan/tilt are camera orientation changes; dolly/truck should produce appropriate depth/parallax; zoom changes scale without the same parallax. Simulate these honestly in a flat/2.5D rig; no fake sweeping 3D rotation from one front-facing sprite.
- Avoid default shaky camera. At a giant footstep, use only a brief small camera displacement synchronized to the impact and decaying smoothly, while props/foliage react independently.
- At portal crossing, lock the camera: it MUST NOT drift/cut/zoom in ways that disguise the threshold or spatial mismatch.
- During the chase maintain a consistent follow relationship without jitter, and keep both actor direction and closing distance readable. Stop the camera movement as narrative tension resolves.

### D3. Editing and continuity

- Observe 180-degree and screen-direction continuity by default, including when camera angle changes. Keep the mother screen RIGHT of the human during the westward chase unless geography is re-established.
- Use matched eyelines, match-on-action and consistent hand-to-prop attachment. Don't restart an arm gesture or repeat a step accidentally across edits.
- Avoid near-identical back-to-back framings that produce accidental jump cuts; change angle/size enough to be purposeful, using the 30-degree guideline as a useful convention rather than universal constraint.
- Use eye-trace: guide audience from egg at old frame to egg/observer in new frame instead of teleporting focus to an unrelated screen corner. Keep points of interest in nearby screen regions where sensible.
- Cut on completed information or motivated movement, not arbitrarily every 1.5 seconds. Leave time to recognize portal crossing, egg catch, hatch, maternal recognition and final snout.
- Edit animation in an animatic before labor-intensive clean art; leave optional handles for editorial timing adjustments.

### D4. Lighting, color and material

- Establish forest daylight from one side and portal cyan as a localized motivated light source; shadows and contact respond consistently.
- Make an eight-beat **color script** with palette/value keys: discovery bright natural + portal cyan; egg delicate warm contrast; mother arrival stronger dark-vs-light; chase dynamic foreground separation; hatch soft concentrated warmth; reunion natural inviting warmth; baby follower mixed playful cyan; apartment cool-neutral interrupted by eerie portal glow.
- Use value hierarchy before saturated accents. Don't overheat every scene or make all paper layers equal brightness/contrast.
- Use depth layering and restrained parallax tied to camera motion; background movement must not visually overpower character action.
- Maintain stable paper grain scale, figure designs, floor baseline and shadow directions between shots. Avoid excessive texture flickering and sharpen/blur artifacts.

### D5. Vertical/mobile framing

Design natively for 1080x1920, not as a crop from landscape. In this project, protect top 170px and bottom 260px plus a conservative right side from essential captions/props/face detail. **These numbers are internal heuristics, not a claim about official organic Shorts safe zones.** Validate against actual YouTube app UI. Text remains secondary to character story. Don't cover an egg-catching hand with subtitles. Brand signature THE CURIOSITY ARCHIVE is small, well aligned, never warped or misrepresented.

## E. Sound direction — treat as an equal storytelling department

### E1. Audio narrative palette

**Diegetic forest:** low breezes, soft leaves, insects, occasional birds, subtle near vs far layers. Offscreen giant footfalls appear before mother, moving closer and brighter/more forceful as she approaches.

**Human:** light ground-specific steps; subtle paper clothing/rustle; controlled breaths, small delighted laugh, gasp/recoil, panicked exertion, relieved exhale. No lengthy dialogue; expressive vocalization enough.

**Mother:** heavy multi-component footsteps with synchronized planted contact, audible body/neck shift, resonant exhale/inhalation before roar, and tactile foliage/branch vibration. Ensure a recognizably protective rather than sadistic performance.

**Baby:** tiny shell motion, crack/rattle, first inhale and chirp, small uneven foot taps, affectionate nuzzle and excited scamper. Avoid stock cartoon squeaks on every frame.

**Portal:** coherent recurring motif: airy shimmer + a papery elastic tension note + soft resonant bass as aperture grows. Its pitch/form subtly changes when an oversized snout pushes through in Scene 8; consistent identity across locations.

**Apartment:** indoor room tone with mild reflected acoustics, carpet rather than grass footsteps, muffled external city presence if useful. Location change audible without using intrusive generic transition sound.

### E2. Spotting / cue protocol

Make a spotting sheet BEFORE final animation: cue ID, shot ID, sound type, story reason, on/off-screen source, world position, screen pan, apparent distance, visual trigger event, onset/peak/decay frame, musical relationship, audio file/asset ID and QA status.

Generate actual `event` markers from animation state: `portal_open`, `human_head_visible_inside`, `human_lead_foot_contact`, `human_trail_foot_contact`, `nest_shakes`, `egg_roll_start`, `egg_hands_contact`, `mother_step_contact`, `mother_notices_egg`, `mother_inhale`, `mother_roar`, `human_recoil`, `human_pivot_contact`, `branch_impact`, `shell_crack`, `baby_head_emerge`, `human_full_stop`, `mother_full_stop`, `baby_ground_contact`, `baby_nuzzle_contact`, `human_portal_cross_complete`, `baby_portal_cross_complete`, `mother_scout_portal`, `mother_snout_threshold`. Foley is triggered from these events, not arbitrary hard-coded timers. Sharp contact SFX onset within ±1 frame of the visible contact, unless intentionally designed as preceding or trailing sound.

### E3. Perspective, bridges and acoustic editing

- Use a J-cut to preview mother's approach with distant steps before showing her; hearing comes first and motivates the human looking around.
- Let some ambiences carry over cuts as appropriate (L-cuts), preserving continuous forest geography. Transition to apartment room tone as crossing becomes real, not long before.
- Differentiate acoustic point of hearing: mother footsteps initially distant and dull, then near and detailed; human breath becomes close during panic; shell crack momentarily foreground and intimate.
- Reserve perceptual quiet for revelation: reduce chase score and major impacts around first egg crack so the sound *changes the viewer's attention*; no arbitrary vacuum silence unless subjectively motivated.
- Stereo panning is supportive rather than extreme; retain key event sounds in mono. Listen in headphones, computer, TV speaker and phone, both stereo and mono.

### E4. Score and editorial rhythm

Music must describe the arc rather than plaster over action. Suggested motifs: curiosity = a few plucked, bright tones; danger = low restrained pulse; chase = lively percussion with rests; hatch = pulse drops to expose shell crack and chirp; reunion = warm spare motif; follower = curious motif returns; apartment snout = sudden intake/low swell then a sharply timed stop.

Score only important phrasing; do not force character steps to every musical beat. Let animation performance and Foley lead when competing for clarity. Use nuanced music ducking under meaningful vocalizations and crack/roar where needed. Avoid music-induced jump cuts, oversized risers before every reveal, generic constant whoosh, and excessive bass masking.

### E5. Engineering and mixing

Keep editable stems grouped as `VO_VOCALS`, `FOLEY`, `SFX_DESIGN`, `AMB_FOREST`, `AMB_APARTMENT`, `MUSIC`, `MASTER_STEREO` (and optional M&E). Work internally with 48kHz lossless material; choose final H.264 MP4, progressive 30fps and AAC-LC stereo 48kHz. Set consistent SDR/Rec.709 export tags and avoid successive lossy transcodes.

**Loudness:** no single YouTube-mandated LUFS number exists in its upload guidance. A provisional internal web mix target around -14 to -16 LUFS integrated and a conservative peak ceiling at/below -1 dBTP can be tested, then adjusted for musical style and comfort; do not apply broadcast EBU R128 -23 LUFS to this web release. Measure final true peak/integrated loudness, inspect short-term loudness, check no clipping or unpleasant transient surprises, and audition at low volume on phone speakers and in mono. If any words are spoken, assess intelligibility without subtitles; avoid overcompression, bass-only cues and phase-dependent sounds.

### Nine-second camera lock and composition

**ONE continuous readable medium-wide shot (no cuts).** Perspective is side-on 2.5D, with sufficient slight depth cues to show person behind/in/front of an upright portal aperture. Keep the actual threshold and ground in frame throughout. Human initially fully *inside* the portal: not left outside frame. Portal at LEFT-middle of stage; person emerging toward frame center/right. Set aperture dimensions sufficient for full human height; lower rim rests on ground. The character starts fully masked within the aperture's back/interior layer. Its front rim temporarily occludes body parts according to their progression through the threshold. Use a persistent depth/threshold coordinate rather than an arbitrary opacity fade. Human's feet must land on same world ground plane as portal base.

**Nine-second micro-timeline (270 frames):**
- **F000-F047 (0.00-1.60s)** Establish handmade forest; portal flickers into existence with cyan local light. Grass is displaced; no human outside.
- **F048-F083 (1.60-2.80s)** Human's head/hand appear FROM INSIDE the portal aperture. Eyes glance into the forest, head follows; torso still behind inner plane. Audio: soft interior rustle/breath.
- **F084-F125 (2.80-4.20s)** Hand tests rim; torso leans; lead knee lifts; lead foot crosses a mapped portal plane and lands on real ground outside. Actual contact event and foot compression; weight begins transferring.
- **F126-F191 (4.20-6.40s)** Torso/pelvis clear doorway; trailing foot crosses and lands outside; no disembodied body segments; body stays occlusion-correct as limbs cross. Both feet support settled stance after crossing.
- **F192-F215 (6.40-7.20s)** Character settles with knees and torso, delayed arm movement; breathing. The portal remains open with coherent shading.
- **F216-F269 (7.20-9.00s)** Eyes track huge fern canopy, head and torso follow; small delighted reaction. Tiny camera adjustment allowed only AFTER the entire exit is proven, and only if it improves emphasis. Forest ambience grows in perceptual importance; no dinosaur appears yet.

**Audio map:** 0.00 low breeze + insects; 0.35-1.6 portal tone opening; ~1.6 interior fabric/paper shift; lead/trail foot sounds MUST be generated on actual planted contact frames rather than guessed at 3.7 or 5.8; soft breath as body settles; quiet canopy rustle and curious gasp after eyes move. Music optional/minimal: do not obscure portal mechanics or footsteps.

**Quality tests:** Present labeled stills and adjacent frame sequences at F047/048, head reveal, first foot just before/during/after contact, torso crossing, trailing foot release/plant, completed exit, gaze reaction. Show portal plane and world-floor debug overlay in QA version. Confirm no offscreen entrance, no foot-floating, no source/joint pop, no object-through-rim errors. Listen audio-only for a believable entrance; inspect without audio for action clarity. Fail any critical defect; revise and rerender before initiating the 80-second film.

## H. Production and QC pipeline

**P0 — Preparation:** Build a 1-page beat and world-geography map, eight-beat color script, character design/scale sheet, rig diagram, prop ownership chart, shot list with camera records, 48kHz sound palette and spotting sheet. State any tools/assets missing.

**P1 — Benchmark blocking:** Build a simple monochrome/rough nine-second portal-exit animatic first. Validate action: interior head -> leg -> real ground planted -> hips -> trailing foot -> complete exit -> environment reaction. Keep full shot visible with no cuts.

**P2 — Benchmark finaling:** Add articulated paper-cut rig, portal depth/occlusion, lighting, parallax with limited camera movement, event-anchored Foley and ambience. Export clip + PNG contact sheet + optional annotated depth-plane/debug frames and cue sheet. Run all three QC passes below. Fix and repeat until blockers are zero and no unresolved major quality defects. Only then advance.

**P3 — Full animatic and editorial:** With validated assets, create complete ~80s coarse animatic. Use minimal rough SFX/score to test timing; time the source stimulus before reaction. Silent watch must recover cause/effect of whole film. Review geography, color script and pacing, leaving adequate time for viewers to perceive the egg and baby.

**P4 — Full animation:** Animate scene by scene; before clean-up check primary action and body mechanics; then implement secondary motion and physical interaction; then camera, lighting and comp; then refined sonic treatment and sound bridges. Validate each shot on contact points, occlusion and gaze before marking it ready.

**P5 — Mix and finishing:** Picture lock before final sound spot/score mix; export lossless stems and final stereo. Confirm delivery codec/color/size/fps, audio sync, frame count. Avoid double lossy transcode. Generate per-shot frames and defects.

### Three independent QC gates

**QC-1 Technical:** All files open; correct frame count and source determinism; no corrupt frames; SDR Rec.709 match; color fidelity; stable frame pacing; proper 48kHz sound; no clipping, true-peak overs, artifacts, misaligned subtitles/branding; correct reproducible exports.

**QC-2 Motion & interaction:** Test root continuity, planted foot movement in world coordinates, arm/leg arcs, weight and recoil, fall/stop momentum, gaze and timing, precise collision, parent/child prop transfer, foreground/background occlusion, no scale popping, actual portal crossings, SFX-to-contact sync. Inspect **consecutive frames**, not just six summary screenshots.

**QC-3 Direction, story & mix:** Watch muted, with only SFX/ambience, with final mix, on phone/desktop/headphones, and on stereo/mono. Does the viewer see why everyone acts? Is visual attention directed correctly? Are camera changes motivated? Does the dinosaur visibly close the distance? Is egg/baby state continuous? Do silence and music serve emotional turning points? Does the visual payoff read? No approval with absent action completion.

**Defect log columns:** `scene_id | frame/time | severity(Blocker/Major/Minor) | expected story action | observed rendered evidence | affected source/assets | root cause | code/art/sound fix | regression evidence | status`.

**Mandatory closing statement:** Exactly what was built and rendered; what source checks and viewer checks passed; what remains unresolved; exact filenames and regeneration commands. Never say “perfect”, “cinematic quality passed”, or “bug-free” without independent verified evidence.

**Final director's mandate:** A viewer must clearly SEE the human emerge through the portal, catch an egg, be misunderstood, defend the egg in a real chase, witness a hatching, reunite mother and baby, go back through the portal, and accidentally bring the problem home.
