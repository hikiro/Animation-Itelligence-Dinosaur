# Context rehearsal exercises — transferring the director's judgment

The documents teach *how to decide*, not just which camera or sound effect looks cinematic. Before generating a scene in another project, run these rehearsal tests. Record choices in `06_TEMPLATES/CONTEXT_DIRECTION_RECORD.md`.

## Exercise 1 — One event, three different emotional framings

Event: A distant, heavy footstep is heard before the dinosaur is revealed.

| Desired response | Audience knowledge | Camera choice | Character action | Audio choice | Editorial implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Curiosity | Audience and human equally unaware | Hold wide and reveal trembling leaf | Human looks up, listens | Muffled offscreen low-mid transient + airy decay | Delay source reveal until after attention shift |
| Suspense | Audience knows mother approaches, human doesn't | Show shadow/foot in foreground while human in background | Human continues protecting egg | Near heavy contact + separate distant ambience | Use dramatic irony, then return to human POV |
| Comedy | Audience anticipates absurd size | Hold calm human close-up as cup/fern trembles | Human misunderstands tremor as breeze | Comically underplayed single tiny rumble | Delay huge reveal, then let oversized foot fill frame |

**Pass condition:** The same action can support different meaning through camera and sound *without contradicting the physical event*.

## Exercise 2 — Reactions don't exist without triggers

Write event ordering for mother discovering the egg:
1. Nest empty is visible to her.
2. Her gaze moves to human's hands.
3. Eyes focus on the egg.
4. Posture changes; breath builds.
5. Roar begins.
6. Human's startle follows the roar's audible onset (or an explicitly earlier visible physical trigger).
7. Escape decision follows recognition that safe placement is impossible.

**Reject:** The mother roars before looking; human recoils before the stimulus; score announces emotion too early and overpowers acting.

## Exercise 3 — Camera motion cannot substitute for locomotion

Show the same crossing twice: (a) locked master with mask debug overlay, (b) tighter dramatic view. Check **world-space** first foot contact and root trajectory under both camera treatments. If the locked version fails, reject both. No push-in or whip pan can compensate for a character drawn outside the aperture.

## Exercise 4 — Sound and motion can disagree deliberately, but must tell a coherent story

A distant thud may lead the source image as anticipation. A shell crack should remain audible while the camera moves to the human's eyes. A musical motif may foreshadow trouble without a visible source. Mark each such cue `LEAD_INTENTIONAL`, with audience-knowledge reason. By contrast, an egg catch should be `CONTACT_LOCKED` to the actual moment hands receive the egg.

## Exercise 5 — Review four versions

For each scene watch: (1) muted animation, (2) SFX-only playback, (3) music-only playback, (4) full mix. Answer:
- Muted: Did the concrete action visibly happen, and did it have clear intention and consequence?
- SFX-only: Do event timing, physical scale, location, and material sound credible?
- Music-only: Does the emotional arc support rather than contradict the visual?
- Full mix: Is the audience drawn to the primary event? Are essential cues masked?

## Exercise 6 — Transfer to an unrelated story

A woman sees a dog escape its leash in a town square. Adapt the same algorithm: source of attention, gaze, response, direction, contacts, ground plane, sound perspective, reactions of passersby, and camera axis. Do not copy prehistoric footsteps or portal effects; reuse the *decision method*.
