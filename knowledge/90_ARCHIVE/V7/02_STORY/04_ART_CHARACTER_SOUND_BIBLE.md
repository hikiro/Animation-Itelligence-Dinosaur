# Visual, character and sonic identity bible

## Artistic direction

A colorful original 2.5D cut-paper film: layered handmade shapes, restrained fibrous textures, subtle contact shadow and registration irregularity, readable facial animation, tactile overlap. **This is not a flat slideshow**: all main characters use independently articulated paper layers whose joints are creatively integrated into the costume/body design.

Materials: consistent scale of paper grain; plausible shadow direction; clean scissor/cut edges; warm greens for prehistoric setting, cyan for portal, speckled warm egg, controlled pops of coral/mustard for focus. Keep palette disciplined: maximum visual contrast at active point of attention, not every corner.

## Character model-lock fields

For each character record ID, front/side/three-quarter silhouettes, body length ratios, palette/swatches, paper texture, pivot list, parent rig tree, z-order map, mouth/eyes shape library, planted-foot rules, audio motif, scale at ground plane, and reference thumbnail. Source assets should be kept as editable paths. Do not mix independently generated poses with inconsistent proportions unless manually reconciled.

### Human

Small articulated explorer. Expressive eyes; compact readable face; feet deliberately large enough to see steps on phone. Light and nimble performance; curiosity expressed through asymmetric lean and gaze; fear through whole-body recoil; compassion through carefully supporting egg/baby. Signature sound: small dry leaf/cloth footsteps, breath and restrained comic gasps.

### Mother dinosaur

Large green-ish protective mother (exact hue adjustable), clear head/neck/torso/hip/tail/limb/foot layers and facial gaze. Heavy stride with delayed mass response. A towering shape relative to human but not so giant that mother cannot fit within stage geography. Signature audio: distant rhythmic footfalls, weighted midrange crunch, warm/raspy breathing, one purposeful protective roar and a gentle final exhale. Do not reuse the roar for all emotions.

### Baby dinosaur

Round-soft visual silhouette, related markings to mother, eye contact toward human established in reunion. Small unsteady walks, chirps that signal curiosity/attachment. Sonic identity: soft short pitch-contour chirps and very light paper feet; avoid continuous squeaking.

### Portal

Cool cyan/turquoise luminous dimensional plane with distinct back rim, aperture, frontal lip and ground-contact glow. Animated rim can deform under force; change light/sound when a body partly crosses. Do not hide passage with glowing overlay: light supports masking, not replaces it.

## World sets

- Prehistoric forest: three or more meaningful depth planes with constrained parallax; nest ledge and chase obstacle placed on consistent ground route. Leaves respond locally to contacts.
- Modern apartment: simple shapes and sufficient clear area at portal; visually distinct from forest using geometric decor and dryer acoustics; no competing props during mother reveal.

## Camera language

Exploration uses patient, readable full-body staging; threat uses contrast of foot/mass and reaction eye-lines; chase uses motivated tracking and clear shrinking gap; hatch uses camera stillness and attention to micro-event; reunion gives space for contact and emotional change; end uses withheld information and controlled reveal. See `01_RESEARCH/01_VISUAL_CAMERA_DIRECTION.md` and `05_CONTEXT_COUPLING.md`.

## Motion language

Human's mass/lightness vs mother's heft vs baby's unstable totter; pose-first then dynamics. Joint cover layers are aesthetic seam choices, not masks for incorrect rigging. See `01_RESEARCH/03_MOTION_ACTING_AND_PHYSICS.md`.

## Sound language

Nonverbal storytelling: real/object-based Foley mixed with deliberately stylized dimensional textures, original sparse motifs and selective audio perspective. Each actor/material has a distinguishable sound identity. See `01_RESEARCH/02_SOUND_MUSIC_PERSPECTIVE.md`.

## Brand

Small stable THE CURIOSITY ARCHIVE signature where useful; avoid interfering with important action or phone UI overlays. Do not improvise or distort an official supplied logo: use its correct asset and aspect ratio when supplied. If unavailable, use a temporary text placeholder and explicitly label as provisional, never claim to have faithfully reproduced an unknown mark.
