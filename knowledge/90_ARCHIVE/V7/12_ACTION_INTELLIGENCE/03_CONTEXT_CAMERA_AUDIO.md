# Camera × motion × sound: all three serve dramatic context

## Context-first director's questions

1. What does **the character** know right now? What does **the viewer** know? Those states can differ intentionally.
2. What must be made visible and audible now, and what should be withheld until later?
3. Is this an act of discovery, choice, impact, pursuit, affection, comic reversal or revelation?
4. What is the source and direction of the stimulus? Can it be heard before it is seen?
5. Where does the camera stand relative to the line of action? Will a cut preserve object locations and screen direction?
6. Is sound objective (emitter), subjective (character perception), or symbolic (score/stinger)? Label it.
7. Are our physical responses appropriate to weight and context? How much exaggeration is consistent with style?

## Same dinosaur foot event, three treatments

| Dimension | Suspense | Comedy | Wonder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Story meaning | Danger approaches | Human's overreaction | Encounter an enormous creature |
| Camera | Foreshadow with shadow; hold source offscreen; reveal foot in a low angle | Hold a medium-wide shot including tiny human and giant foot so audience sees the joke | Low-to-high connected reveal with human scale reference |
| Actor | Notice shake → freeze → tense → pivot and escape | Notice → long deadpan pause → oversized leap → recover | Notice → look up slowly → take one careful step toward it |
| Foot/ground | Heavy landing; foliage compresses; delayed ripple | Same physical ground contact but reaction timing stretched for contrast | Massive mass visible without sharp threatening accents |
| Sound | Offscreen heavy footfalls; subtract ambience; low-frequency impact | Stomp, awkward silence, small squeak | Broad resonant impact, wind/forest continuity, restrained musical swell |
| QA | Source danger recognized before flee | Viewer has time to see setup and delayed reaction | Scale recognized without unintended terror |

## Why more effects can worsen a scene

- Adding shake before contact introduces a false cause.
- A tight insert that hides the hand-contact moment can erase the action the insert was supposed to clarify.
- A fast reverse-angle camera cut can invert predator and prey screen positions.
- Repeating impact sounds at a fixed metronome ignores stance/footfall events.
- Full music intensity during a tiny egg crack makes the emotional reversal inaudible.

## Camera selection algorithm

**Start with spatial truth:** world map + subject coordinates + line of action + eyelines. Then identify the shot's dramatic function: reveal information, hide information, track an interaction, or deliver an emotional point of view. Choose shot size and camera motion for that function. If a proposed move destroys the essential contact point, reject it or move the camera. If a deliberate axis crossing occurs, provide re-establishing geography.

## Audio selection algorithm

Build an event registry with `event_id`, `source_actor`, `source_position`, `start_frame`, `contact_frame`, `sound_type`, `acoustic_space`, `viewer_perspective`, `priority` and `license`. An intentionally offscreen footstep may be audible before the dinosaur is pictured, but **only** when the source event actually happens offscreen; a contact Foley sound should align to that event's real frame. Control distance, occlusion, reverb, filtering, and mix balance. If one sound tells the story, carve frequency/time space for it rather than layering more unrelated sounds.

## Sound and camera timing distinctions

- J-cut: next scene's sound precedes its picture, intentionally connecting geography or emotion.
- L-cut: prior scene's sound continues across next shot, intentionally bridging a cut.
- Diegetic sound: belongs to the story world; non-diegetic score need not obey the scene's acoustic physics.
- Sound perspective follows virtual source and listener positions, unless deliberately subjective.
- A camera push-in may coincide with a dramatic realization, not necessarily with an object physically speeding up.
