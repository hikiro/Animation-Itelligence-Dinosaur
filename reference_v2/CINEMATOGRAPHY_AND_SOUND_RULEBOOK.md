# CINEMATIC VISUAL DIRECTION & SOUND DESIGN RULEBOOK
## DINO//DISASTER and future 2D / 2.5D animated films — v2.0 (2026-11-19)

**Purpose.** A practical, research-informed directing and quality-control standard, especially for handmade paper-cut animation made with code-driven tools. This is a production synthesis, not a claim that every creative guideline is an immutable industry regulation.

**Evidence key:** (A) craft convention (may be intentionally broken); (P) chosen project design standard (not universal); (T) technical delivery requirement when specified by destination; (Q) auditable failure condition.

## 1. Governing priority

Story comprehension > physical and spatial continuity > character performance > audiovisual clarity > visual style polish > spectacle. An attractive camera move is a failure if it conceals a required action. An appealing soundscape is a failure if it masks the event the audience needs to understand.

Every *character* action: stimulus -> notice -> assess -> anticipate -> act -> impact -> recover. Every *camera* action: narrative question -> intended focus -> framing/movement -> new knowledge/feeling. Every *sound* action: acoustic source or deliberate subjective cue -> onset/perspective -> narrative function -> decay or transition.

## 2. Visual composition and staging

1. **Readability and emphasis (A/Q):** At each beat there is one principal visual idea. Use silhouette, value contrast, size, movement, eye direction, and negative space to control attention. Test grayscale, thumbnail size, and muted playback; avoid simultaneous competing focal points.
2. **Shot sizes (A):** Establishing/wide communicates geography and relative scale; medium shows action and interaction; close-up shows perception/emotion; insert shows critical object states (egg rolling, shell crack); reaction shot proves thought or consequence. Do not overuse inserts that hide actual action completion.
3. **Camera angles (A):** Eye level supports empathy; low angle enlarges mother dinosaur; high angle can communicate the human's vulnerability; POV ties viewer to an observer; extreme angle only if motivated and spatially clear.
4. **Look room / head room (A):** Give a moving or gazing subject appropriate space in front, preserve limbs and props through contact actions, and avoid cutting at distracting joints. Human face, eyes, egg, and mother must remain visually separable.
5. **Composition tools (A):** Rule of thirds, leading lines, framing within framing, geometric balance, depth planes, and color contrast are options, not dogmas. A centered portal may communicate an unusual doorway more clearly than rigid thirds.
6. **Paper-cut material consistency (P):** Fibrous paper texture in world space; distinct cut borders, contact shadows, registration offsets and restrained halftone; consistent light direction and scale of grain. Avoid generating different visual designs for the same character in different shots.
7. **Color script (A/P):** Discovery: cool cyan portal against warm green forest; alarm: green desaturates, deeper shadows with brief warm accents; chase: high readability and directional accents, not random red flashing; hatch: warm localized eggshell light; reunion: welcoming natural colors; apartment: cooler geometric domestic palette interrupted by portal cyan. Preserve skin/characters/egg contrast.
8. **Lighting logic (A):** Establish a primary daylight direction plus a portal emissive contribution. Character cutout shadows and foreground shadows must respond consistently. Dramatic lighting is allowed, but the cause of a significant light change should be story-motivated.
9. **Depth (A/P):** Foreground fern occlusion, middleground action, background trees/sky. Track layers with scale-appropriate parallax and avoid all layers moving at the same speed. In 2.5D, don't fake a deep 3D orbital camera if there are no alternate character views.
10. **Vertical composition (P):** 1080x1920, critical action and faces away from top 170px and bottom 260px, plus reserve a conservative right-side buffer for overlays. These are project heuristics, **not official organic Shorts safe-zone specifications**. Verify with a device/platform UI overlay and keep captions editable.

## 3. Cinematography and movement

11. **Motivated camera (A/Q):** Prior to each move write: target; purpose; start/end composition; velocity; depth effect; handoff into next shot. Camera moves to reveal, follow, emphasize scale, or shift emotional identification—not to fill visual silence.
12. **Move vocabulary (A):** Pan/tilt rotates view; dolly/truck changes viewpoint and parallax; zoom changes image scale without true viewpoint displacement; tracking maintains a chosen relationship to moving action; crane/boom alters elevation; locked-off holds let performance command attention. A pseudo-zoom of a flat composite cannot create realistic parallax.
13. **Camera energy by emotion (P):** Stable and observant during entrance; discreet forward emphasis when egg is noticed; low-angle and scale-contrasting reveal for mother; side-on tracking through chase; locked/near-static for first hatch and reunion; close withheld information for comic payoff. Avoid default shake.
14. **Camera shake (A/P):** Small, physically caused displacements for major dinosaur impacts, with fast response and controlled settle. The world and camera may respond differently; never apply repetitive per-frame random shake, especially near subtitles.
15. **Camera speed and blur (A/P):** Smooth acceleration and deceleration when motivated, controlled handheld-like movement only when needed, realistic shutter-inspired blur on rapid motion, no blur masking pose errors. Lock a consistent rendering approach for the film; test 30fps capture on 1s versus a deliberately stepped paper-cut movement cycle on 2s.
16. **Screen axis and eyelines (A/Q):** Define story geography: portal WEST (screen left), nest EAST, mother approaches from farther EAST; chase WEST. Keep mother on the human's right in chase coverage unless a visible neutral shot or move re-establishes the axis. Close-ups must maintain human eye direction toward the mother and mother's downward gaze toward the egg.
17. **Continuity edit (A/Q):** 180-degree line and screen direction; matched eyelines; matched object locations; match-on-action across the edit; deliberate 30-degree/shot-size difference to avoid unintentional jump cuts; maintain motion continuity and prop hands. These are cinematic conventions, not laws; purposeful disorientation may break them with a clear story rationale.
18. **Eye-trace across cuts (A):** Place the new focus near where the viewer was already looking, or provide a motion/contrast cue to redirect attention; measure where egg, human eyes, dinosaur eyes are on last/first frames of adjacent shots.
19. **Editorial pacing (A/P):** Allow establishing orientation before revealing detail. Hold anticipation before big reveals; don't cut away during decisive physical contacts. Use fast cutting for the chase only if the action direction, timing, and object states remain readable; longer takes for the baby reveal and reunion.
20. **Action completion (Q):** No critical act is relegated to an offscreen jump cut: portal entry, catch, branch contact, hatch, hand-to-ground transfer and snout passing through the doorway must be visible.
21. **Camera and puppet coordinate systems (Q):** World-space stage independent from camera x/y/scale and any parallax layer factors. Feet measured in world coordinates; actor root orientation never changes merely to compensate for screen translation. Track object ownership and explicit near/far occlusion states.

## 4. Sound concept and acoustical perspective

22. **Spotting session (A/Q):** Before animation finaling, list the dramatic event, on-screen acoustic source, needed sound, start/onset/contact/decay, perspective and priority. Use an event/cue sheet with timecodes and exact contact-frame mapping after render.
23. **Track groups (A):** Character vocalizations/breaths; footsteps and surface Foley; prop Foley (egg, shell, branch); designed portal and giant footfall effects; forest/apartment ambiences; music; optional narrator/dialogue. Export distinct stems for revision.
24. **Diegetic logic (A):** Offscreen footsteps must originate from the unseen mother and get closer before reveal. A visible step lands once; don't double the heavy impact on an unrelated frame. A shell crack must not sound like a rock smash; give props distinctive timbres.
25. **Aural perspective (A):** Near sounds louder, more detailed and present; distant sounds quieter, less bright, embedded in ambience. Perspective also follows editorial viewpoint: the frightened human may hear the dinosaur's approaching breath exaggerated subjectively. Return to normal perspective after the moment.
26. **Foley as physical contact (A/Q):** Human feet light, papery/grassy; mother steps heavy with impact, ground vibration, leaf rattle and low-frequency support that still reads on phone speakers. Use texture/body on each stride, synced to contact, not clock ticks. Hand grip and egg transfers get subtle rustle/tactile sound.
27. **Designed sound identity (A/P):** Portal = airy spectral shimmer plus paper-like tension; mother = textured breath, resonance, body/footfall, not only a generic roar; baby = warm small chirps, lightly squeaky body movement; egg = delicate porcelain-like shell snaps with damp warmth; paper forest = soft fiber/rustle.
28. **Ambience continuity (A/Q):** Forest beds (wind/insects/birds/foliage) persist naturally beneath cuts. Change audible density during danger without arbitrarily muting every environmental sound. Apartment room tone is clearly different but spatially stable.
29. **Music dramaturgy (A/P):** Sparse curious motif -> subtle tension for egg wobble -> low, restrained pulse under mother recognition -> action rhythm for chase -> music drops toward hatch -> warm simple motif for reunion -> whimsical short punctuation -> tension/stop for final portal snout. Never continuous relentless beat.
30. **Sound bridges and editing (A):** Use J-cuts for approaching offscreen mother footfalls before picture reveal; L-cuts to carry ambience or emotional reaction through edits. Don't make a sound bridge contradict shot location/time.
31. **Silence / selective reduction (A):** At first hatch, briefly reduce music and large action effects to expose shell crack and baby chirp; maintain intentional environmental room/forest tone unless an explicitly subjective silence is desired.
32. **Spatial stereo and mono (A/Q):** Pan distinct moving diegetic sources subtly, but preserve important sounds and any dialogue in mono. Check folding to mono: no phase cancellation or missing portal/character sounds. Treat phones/headphones/TV speakers as actual distribution environments.
33. **Hierarchy and masking (A/Q):** Prioritize comprehension: character-relevant event > important voice/gasp/chirp > contact Foley > ambience/score. Make space by arrangement, EQ and gentle automation/ducking—not by flattening everything with limiting. Avoid constant high-pitched whooshes.
34. **Dynamics (A/P):** Use dynamic contrast for surprise and tenderness while controlling painful transients. Test at low playback volume; don't count on sub-bass to carry a footstep or giant dinosaur scale on mobile.
35. **Event-based sync (Q):** Emit named animation contacts/reveals (e.g. `left_foot_contact`, `egg_landed_in_hands`, `mother_roar_onset`, `shell_first_crack`, `portal_cross_threshold`). Trigger corresponding audio from these event frames; verify sync within 1 frame at 30fps for visible sharp contacts. Deliberate prelap anticipations must be explicitly labeled.
36. **Sound accessibility (A/Q):** Confirm viewers can understand plot both muted and without captions, and any dialogue or intelligible text is comprehensible on a typical phone. Provide captions for spoken words if added. Separate sound effects from words; avoid intense flashing or abrupt overpowering spikes.

## 5. Technical audio and video policies

- **Creative stereo web master (P):** 48kHz/24-bit PCM stems, final encoded audio 48kHz AAC-LC stereo in MP4/H.264. Apply appropriate loudness measurement; an *internal web mix starting target* around -14 to -16 LUFS integrated and a conservative ceiling at or below -1 dBTP may be used provisionally, but these are **not YouTube-mandated loudness targets**. Audition, measure and revise for the actual story/dynamic range.
- **Broadcast version (T, only if commissioned):** Follow the actual broadcaster's written delivery sheet; for example BBC/EBU R128 television programming can require around -23 LUFS program loudness and true-peak constraints. Do not casually apply a broadcast target to YouTube or vice versa.
- **Production versioning (P):** Master stereo, separate dialogue/vocals, Foley, designed FX, ambience and music stems, plus an M&E export if useful. Do not repeatedly transcode lossy audio during composition.
- **Technical check (Q):** No clipped samples/inter-sample overs, discontinuous loops, audible clicks, transient clipping, distorted bass on a phone, mismatched channel polarity, or drift between exported audio/video. Confirm sRGB/Rec.709 SDR mappings, normal frame count, and A/V sync.

## 6. Applied story geography, visual grammar and audio arc

Stage orientation fixed for Episodes 1's prehistoric sequence: portal at screen LEFT, egg nest to RIGHT, mother farther RIGHT. Human journeys to nest rightward, rescues egg, then is chased toward LEFT. The portal remains open throughout; it never shuts before the characters return. A neutral geography-restoring shot must be included before any deliberate screen-axis reversal.

| Scene | Main purpose | Shot/Camera grammar | Visual lighting/composition | Audio story function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01, 0-9s | Prove true portal exit | Single readable medium-wide side-on take, restrained post-exit follow | Grounded blue aperture; forest scale; silhouette; threshold visible | Air/portal shimmer -> contact step -> curious forest |
| 02, 9-19s | Reveal danger and compassion | Wide nest geography -> insert roll -> medium sustained catch | Egg isolated by contrast; clear down/forward arc | Two early heavy distant thuds, egg rattle, small gasp, catch |
| 03, 19-30s | Establish mother's mistaken belief | Low angle reveal -> mother POV toward empty nest -> egg in hands -> reaction | Mother/egg geography clear; darker relief | Offscreen approach, heavy step contact, inhale, roar, recoil |
| 04, 30-46s | Pursuit, physical stakes | Side follow WEST; medium-wide for gait; purposeful close contact details | Keep both visible, readable chase corridor | Sync footprints, rising score, branch snap, egg rustle, stumble |
| 05, 46-56s | Transform threat into revelation | Decelerate camera to rest; hand/egg insert -> held two-shot | Warm value around egg; subdued background | Music drop, shell crack, chirp, brakes tail-rustle |

## 7. Nine-second benchmark — **must pass before the 80-second film**

Single continuous side-on camera, no cuts that can hide incorrect emergence. Portal bottom at world-ground baseline; scaled aperture large enough for the human. Human must begin entirely occluded inside/behind the portal's interior. Camera remains nearly locked until after crossing.

**0.00-1.60s:** Ambient forest and glowing portal construction; grass responds; no human visible outside aperture.
**1.60-2.80s:** Head and hand become visible *within* portal aperture; head scans, shoulders follow gaze; body is still inside and correctly masked.
**2.80-4.20s:** Forward lean and knee lift; leading foot crosses threshold (in depth and projected screen space), plants ahead of portal; floor-contact sound event emitted at actual contact.
**4.20-6.40s:** Torso and hips cross through; COM moves over support; trailing foot lifts, clears threshold, lands outside; occlusion front/back changes only as each body part crosses.
**6.40-7.20s:** Character settles, adjusts stance, breathes; portal continues emitting appropriate environmental light.
**7.20-9.00s:** Eyes lift to giant plant canopy, head follows, chest opens, slight curious smile. Ambient forest becomes subjectively audible. No other character enters yet.

**Mandatory frame evidence:** storyboard and actual frames just before and after first appearance; leading-foot crossing and planted support; hip crossing; trailing-foot release and plant; final full exit; seamless 30fps clip and isolated sound stems.

**Benchmark rejection triggers:** person enters from screen edge; under/over/apart from portal; portal floats above foot-level; occlusion reversed; root jumps; foot skating; hand clipping; offset audio; camera cut conceals missing crossing; gaze and reactions non-causal.

## 8. QC signoff procedure

Technical pass: decode all frames, A/V sample rate and timestamps, color/format checks, no missing assets. Motion pass: inspect consecutive render frames around contacts and pose switches, no world-space foot drift, object/state continuity, weight/recoil. Director pass: silent thumbnail viewing (story readable), audio-only listening (cause/proximity intelligible), stereo and mono mobile playback, eye-trace and geography, emotional pacing. Record severity (Blocker, Major, Minor), timestamp, evidence path, root cause, exact fix, rerender and regression result. Zero blockers required to approve; major flaws must be resolved or explicitly documented as accepted tradeoffs. Never label a draft a completed professional film merely because it produced an MP4.
