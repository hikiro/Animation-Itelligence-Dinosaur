# Camera and sound serve context, not a default effect stack

## Camera
Define audience knowledge and geographic axis before moving the camera. Use shot size to determine what information is available. Camera movement should reveal, follow, connect, conceal with purpose or change emotional perspective. Maintain consistent perceived scale and screen direction except for deliberate, legible disorientation. For a large animal, use environment and opposing actor in frame to provide scale; for intimate acting, protect gaze and microgesture visibility.

## Sound
Identify an acoustic source, material, event ID and listener perspective for each cue. A large dinosaur footstep may need a short contact transient, delayed debris, room/forest tail and perspective as it approaches; these are layers, not a stock "heavy" setting. Music may withdraw to foreground the hatchling's quiet chirp. Silence is a designed contrast, not a missing file.

## Coupled event plan example
**Event:** egg cracks during pursuit. **Intention:** shift attention from threat to new life. **Body:** human decelerates, protects egg, looks down; dinosaur perceives chirp and brakes. **Camera:** stabilize/close on shell then reaction. **Sound:** reduce chase score, place shell crack correctly, reveal tiny chirp; stop footfalls after support changes. **QA:** the viewer identifies the crack as the trigger of both characters' behavior.

## Avoid cross-department contradictions
Don't use heroic music over credible terror unless intentional irony. Don't zoom away at contact when action understanding depends on it. Don't synchronize an impact to an arbitrary beat if the visual contact is elsewhere. No audio can substitute for physically missing contact.

**Sources:** SRC-001 general performance; V7 archived cinematography/sound rulebook includes further citations and expanded practice. The event plans here are authored hypotheses needing render/listening tests.
