# Controlled test — one physical event across threat/comedy/wonder/tenderness

## Scientific question

Can dramatic **context** alter the audience's understanding of the same underlying foot-ground contact without inventing new physics? The foot touches at the same world location and time; camera, acting and audio alter the meaning.

## Controlled variables

- Physical event: large dinosaur foot steps down on a ground patch next to a small human.
- Event graph: offscreen approach → visible foot descent → actual ground contact → ground deformation/vegetation movement → human awareness → human response.
- Same source coordinate and contact frame (`f=45` at 30 fps) in all versions. Same ground and character scale.

## Variables to change

| Context | Character interpretation | Acting | Camera | Audio | Viewer test question |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Threat | Danger | startled recoil, guarded retreat | looming scale then reaction | thud + bass body, ambience dip | What threatens whom? |
| Comedy | Surprise without lasting harm | held pose, exaggerated double take | readable fixed wide | dry plop/squeak and gap | What makes the moment funny? |
| Wonder | Astonishing new discovery | look up, relaxed approach | slow reveal | material impact + gentle harmonic swell | Why is the human fascinated? |
| Tenderness | Familiar, gentle presence | soft shoulders, open hand, mutual response | close stable two-shot | softened material + near gentle breath | Who trusts whom? |

## Intended staged outputs

- `stomp_context_contact_sheet.png`: original three/four-column *schematic storyboards*, not sourced third-party boards.
- Optional rendered tests require real audio and motion files with measured contact frame and at least two independent viewer interpretations to reach L5.

## Five test instruments

**A. Story-causality review:** Can reviewer name stimulus, interpretation, decision and visible aftermath?

**B. Silent-first test:** Without sound, distinguish danger/comedy/wonder/tenderness from body and camera alone. If not, motion lacks adequate performance.

**C. Audio-only test:** Which source, distance and event would listener infer? If ambiguous, mark L4 audio failure.

**D. Frame-evidence test:** Contact occurs at fixed frame across contexts; foot and shadow align; reactions do not anticipate stimulus unless intentionally performed as such.

**E. Blind audience check:** Randomize labels and ask independent reviewers to choose intended emotion; provide N, counts and confusions. Not performed as part of static dataset generation.

Do not equate an appealing storyboard sheet with the result of a listener or viewer experiment.

## Original storyboard illustration (V6)

`stomp_four_contexts_original_storyboard.png` is an **original schematic 4×4 contact sheet** created to teach how the same contact could be staged under distinct intentions. It passed a manual image review for **pre-contact separation and contact ordering** after correcting an initial defect: the first draft accidentally showed the foot touching the floor in the nominal approach panel. The corrected image shows an airborne foot during approach and grounded foot at contact. A second correction fixed pre-noticing eye direction. **Camera angles, animation timing, and actual sound have NOT been implemented or tested in this static board** despite their proposed direction in the lesson.

Open defects: all four variants share near-identical staging; the cartoon foot is only a symbol; no source perspective tests, no blind audience survey, and no contact-frame animation. Do not promote this storyboard to L5.
