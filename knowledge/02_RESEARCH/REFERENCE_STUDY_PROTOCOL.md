# Reference-study protocol — scripts, drawn storyboards, footage and rigs

**Purpose:** convert real productions and observational anatomy into reusable contextual knowledge without inventing unseen details or exceeding rights/permissions.

## Acquire and classify
Record asset title, author/owner, source URL, retrieval date, rights, accessibility, version, runtime/page count, medium (`script`, `drawn_board`, `animatic`, `layout`, `rig`, `finished_shot`, `animal_reference`, `audio_stem`), confidence and relation to linked assets. Prefer matched script–board–animatic–final pairs. Keep direct observation separate from creator commentary, from our interpretation, and from proposed code.

## Analyze a script and storyboard pair
- Segment story at observable beats: perception, goal, decision, action, outcome, aftereffect.
- In drawn boards record panel/page, characters, blocking, eyelines, foreground/background, shot size, camera direction, action arrows and source sounds.
- In animatic note beats, frame durations, editor transitions and audio placement; not every panel is a literal keyframe.
- In final shot distinguish what was changed from the storyboard and whether there is evidence why. Avoid claiming intent without interviews/annotations.
- Score viewer information before and after each shot: what is known, what is revealed, what remains ambiguous, deliberate open loops and payoff.
- Write a small original paraphrase rather than reproducing entire copyrighted documents; link to permitted source and keep any user-supplied files private in their archive.

## Study creatures and size
- Identify actual species/body plan; record camera angle, frame rate if known, surface, speed, slope and movement mode.
- Mark foot contact/lift, joint bend, pelvis/shoulder trajectory, head/tail delays and start/stop mechanics.
- Distinguish visual estimates from measured numerical tracking; don't assert physical weight from appearance alone.
- Record camera perspective artifacts, footage edits and unknown scale references.
- For extinct/fictional animals identify which elements are inference or deliberate stylization.
- Convert observation into a control hypothesis, then implement and falsify it through short rig tests.

## Explain in ordinary language
For every observation write: “When [stimulus] happens in [context], [actor] first [perceives/prepares], then [visible action], which changes [world/relationship], while [camera/audio] help reveal [audience information].” Follow with actual code/rig controls and a failure counterexample.

## Audit requirements
Each research note links stable IDs and evidence level, records the original visual or text source, states missing context, distinguishes measured vs estimated, and includes an appropriate regression question. Research findings alone remain L1/L2; code and render tests advance separately.
