# Three source-based script/board studies — and what remains an interpretation

## Case A — A script conveys a changing decision

**Source:** BBC Doctor Who S10E1 shooting script, PDF p.18, scene immediately before scene 29. Direct PDF text and screenshot inspected.

**Observed script-level event (paraphrase):** a person starts walking away, looks back, recognizes that someone is frightened; the second person appears to decide to make a request. Later the pair move through campus, with one trying to sustain conversation.

**What is directly supported:** departure/reconsideration, attention to another person's expression, visible hesitation/decision and spoken request. **Not supplied:** frame count, lens, exact eye movement latency, hand pose, numeric easing, audio mix.

**Our directing hypothesis (not a source quote):** board departing step → glance → recognition → stop/turn → reciprocal eye contact → request. If illustrating nonverbally, a slow backward turn and one held glance could make concern legible. Verify only by animatic viewer test.

**Transfer to DINO:** mother sees empty nest → looks at egg → incorrectly interprets action → changes from searching to confronting. The mother’s belief must differ from audience knowledge that the egg was rescued.

## Case B — Drawn storyboard sequences encode spatial staging

**Source:** Bournemouth University *Once Upon a Time in Animation*, PDF p.13, six-panel board. Direct screenshot inspected.

**Observed:** a telescope and watching character appear in initial panel; another creature enters the spatial relationship; subsequent panels alter framing and expression to develop the encounter. Wide, medium and closer views organize viewer attention.

**What remains unknown:** exact durations, complete movement in between drawings, separate sound cue timings, author choices beyond published panels. A storyboard is *not* a verified animation.

**Our annotation:** panel-to-panel relation should include `actor visible`, `attention target`, `viewer knowledge`, `event transition`, `shot size`, `screen direction`; gaps need hypothesis flags not fabricated keyframes.

**Transfer to DINO:** sequence of human head within portal → crossing threshold → planted lead foot → pelvis and trailing foot clearing doorway → discovery reaction. One attractive board of a human *next to* the portal cannot establish that traversal occurred.

## Case C — A drawn production sheet provides context besides pictures

**Source:** Bournemouth University PDF p.14, *Friends & Heroes* storyboard sheet. Direct screenshot inspected.

**Observed:** three horizontally arranged, dramatic panels with header fields (episode/sequence/shot) and reserved dialogue/production rows.

**Inference:** those metadata fields enable shot identity and revision tracking; their presence does not prove the content of unclear handwritten notes. OCR/unreadable marks must not be guessed.

**Production schema lesson:** Source annotations should identify shot ID, frame/scene context and what is actually visible. Do not rely on isolated image thumbnails without their sequence and production annotations.

## Case D — Script information is not final animation geometry

**Source:** BBC Doctor Who S10E1 script, p.59, escape scene with blocked doorway.

**Observed paraphrase:** two characters encounter an aperture blocked by rubble and squeeze through it.

**Why it matters:** a naive generated sequence might slide bodies behind a foreground wall. Our action completion test demands parts of the body cross a consistent boundary while the environment occludes correctly.

## Case E — Sound perspective carries source and character location

**Source:** BBC Radio Drama Scene Format pp.1–3. Directly read. **Observed:** instruction distinguishes intimate/close vocal perspective, off-mic, voiceover and distorted speech. Musical transitions have specified functions.

**Our implementation hypothesis:** in animation, an offscreen dinosaur footfall could precede visual revelation to establish threat; sound direction and room/forest response must remain consistent with eventual on-screen source. A generic bass hit cannot alone establish where the dinosaur is.

## Evaluation checklist for any subsequent real source

1. Access permission and direct source inspection recorded?
2. Can we distinguish source facts from annotation hypotheses?
3. Do script, drawing, animatic and finished shot truly refer to same project/shot?
4. Are image sequences complete or just curated stills?
5. Does sound evidence exist, or only a description?
6. Do observations specify context (preceding beat/character belief/recipient)?
7. Are exact timecode and frame indices derived from media rather than invented?
8. Can independent readers reproduce the observation at the URL/page?
