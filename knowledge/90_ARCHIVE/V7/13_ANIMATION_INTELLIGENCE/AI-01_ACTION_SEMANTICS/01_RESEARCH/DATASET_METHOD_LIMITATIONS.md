# Dataset method, validation levels and sampling limitations

## What “large” means here

The corpus is intentionally **broad in vocabulary and structured context coverage**; this is not the same as thousands of real, independently annotated film shots. A large lexicon is useful for retrieval, but does not establish that a language-to-animation system understands action semantics.

The counted corpus comprises lexeme candidates, synthetic contextual interpretations, authored action-case blueprints, source notes ambiguity readings, and forty unscored held-out director test cases. Only the source notes have outside-source provenance; no actor or audience comprehension scores are invented. The held-out evaluation list contains examples beyond the four context presets, such as suspense and explicit realism-versus-stylization conflicts. No system has yet been scored against them. The split labels do not create a trained model or demonstrate generalization.

## Origin classes

| Origin | Evidence | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|
| `author_curated_vocabulary` | Authored candidate term | Retrieval and vocabulary expansion |
| `synthetic_template_expansion_NOT_A_REAL_SCRIPT` | Generated by four context profiles | Contrast prompts and hypothesis generation |
| `designed_contextual_example` | Specific authored event blueprint | Storyboard/rig experiments with manual review |
| `source observation` | Paraphrased from directly inspected source location | Principle provenance, cross-check against source |

## Split strategy and leakage

Lexicon and its contextual variants share a `lemma` split; action variants share the same base action split. Do not evaluate memorized action variants in training and claim generalization to the same base on test. This naive split is only a starting reproducibility aid; author, title, project, era, style, language and source-level leakage require stronger grouping once real shots are collected.

## Mechanical checks versus expert validity

**Mechanical validators** can prove unique IDs, valid JSON, required field presence, source references, four contexts per term/action, and no base-action split leakage. They cannot certify emotional legibility, how a reader interprets a drawn pose, or audio quality.

**Domain review** should involve at least one animator/storyboard artist and one independent viewer. Future blind tests must present unlabeled silent animatics and ask viewers what happened, why, and what emotion was intended. Audio-only tests ask what source/location was inferred. Report actual sample sizes and agreement; none exist yet for V6.

## Copyright and research integrity

- A web page with storyboards is not permission to copy its images into a dataset.
- BBC scripts and Disney/Pixar sketches are used only as cited, paraphrased case observations with page/section references.
- No source images, long script passages, or audio files from these sources are embedded.
- Blender Studio **Spring** materials may have a CC license for specific assets, but production archive availability and per-asset use must be checked independently.
- Search previews and metadata do not count as having inspected a full scene.

## Known bias and limitations

English-dominant vocabulary; designed cartoon/adventure prompts; little documentary/dialogue-heavy acting data; strong dependence on DINO examples; no gesture capture, actor annotations, perception study, synchronized source media, or AE runtime test. Corpus contains compound and stylized words that require normalization for natural-user search.

## Next evaluation loop

Collect a small properly documented **real shot pair** (script sentence + drawn board + finished shot) where rights permit. Annotate at least two independent interpretations, adjudicate disagreements, render a non-copied comparable scene, measure action comprehension, then add a validated reference record. Do this repeatedly instead of inflating synthetic rows without source evidence.
