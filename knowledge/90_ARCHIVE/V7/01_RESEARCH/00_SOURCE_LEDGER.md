## Research synthesis, not literal claims from sources

- `R01`: **Context triple** — for every story beat define camera's narrative question, actor's intention/force, and sound's source/POV/priority; test whether all three communicate the *same* event without redundancy.
- `R02`: **Action contract** — major verb must be visually completed with contact or threshold proof, not merely implied by narration or effect.
- `R03`: **Three listening perspectives** — objective environment, character subjective hearing, audience editorial anticipation; label which is used and when changed.
- `R04`: **Camera cost rule** — a moving camera adds parallax/scale/change of focus, so it must earn that perceptual cost by revealing, following, contrasting or reframing.
- `R05`: **Single dominant idea per beat** — do not maximize movement, captions, score and camera energy at once; direct attention with deliberate hierarchy.
- `R06`: **Event-sourced cue sheet** — derive footsteps, impacts, contacts and their sound from frame-level events; allow intentional prelaps/offscreen cues with documented exceptions.
- `R07`: **No-media-no-verification** — a source file, proposed prompt or successful encoding check does not prove the performance was good. Inspect rendered evidence.

These are original production heuristics synthesized for this project, not imposed studio rules.

## Method limits

This research is a targeted synthesis of credible production workflows plus the user's three articles; it is **not** a systematic review of every cinema school or TV broadcaster's standards. Terms such as 30-degree rule, rule of thirds, three-point lighting, and classic 180-degree blocking are useful conventions with legitimate exceptions. Web metadata can change; a future agent should verify technical specifications against the target distribution platform instead of treating 2026 links as permanent.
