# Visual direction, camera, staging and editing — working rulebook

**Evidence anchors:** S01/S02/S06/S07/S08/S09/S10 in `00_SOURCE_LEDGER.md`. **Project translation:** Our camera must clarify action, sustain empathy and avoid hiding physical events. Camera instructions below are practical conventions/choices, not laws of optics or cinema.

## Before choosing a shot: 10 questions

1. Whose story moment is this — human, mother, baby, or audience with more information than them?
2. What new knowledge must the viewer gain during this shot?
3. What is the dominant visual subject: face, hand contact, portal aperture, dinosaur scale, or geography?
4. What must remain in frame for the decisive action to be proven?
5. What is the current emotional distance: curious observation, vulnerability, terror, sympathy, intimacy, comedy?
6. Where did the viewer look at the end of the previous shot, and where should attention go next?
7. Which side of the action axis is the camera on, and what screen directions are established?
8. Is there a physical viewpoint change or only a flat crop/zoom? What parallax could it plausibly produce?
9. Is camera motion necessary, or can the actor's performance carry the moment?
10. What sound can lead, accompany or follow the revelation without confusing the source?

## Shot-size grammar (functions, not automatic prescriptions)

| Frame | Core narrative job | DINO application | Misuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| Establishing / extreme wide | Where are we? Space, environmental scale, orientation | Forest and portal before arrival | Portal too small to prove crossing |
| Wide/long | Show full body and geography, route and physical interactions | Opening portal crossing, chase obstacles | Tiny facial acting unreadable on phone |
| Full / medium-long | Show body, hands and feet simultaneously | First foot plant, catching egg, baby placement | Cropping ground contact |
| Medium / two-shot | Character relations and exchange | Human vs mother and egg; reunion | Lacking face/prop contrast |
| Close-up | Thought, gaze, sudden information | Dinosaur eyes see egg; human hears crack | Replacing critical whole-body action |
| Extreme close / insert | Specific state change | Shell crack, portal rim deform | Accidental jump cut that hides event |
| POV/reaction | Establish psychological perspective | Human sees giant foot, mother sees egg | POV mismatch or eye-line contradiction |

## Height, angle and lens intent

- **Eye-level** feels reciprocal/observational; use for curiosity and reunion.
- **Low angle** emphasizes mother dinosaur mass and perceived danger; retain leg-to-ground scale evidence.
- **High angle** may emphasize human vulnerability, but avoid shrinking egg visibility and confusing direction.
- **Foreground occlusion** adds 2.5D depth only when layer boundaries behave correctly. Do not fake 3D camera orbit around a paper cutout with no side/back asset views.
- **Wide-angle metaphor** near the mother can enhance dominance, but severe perspective distortion would be false if generated by simple 2D scaling; choose lens language feasible in the renderer.
- **Deep focus** often best for portal and contact proof; shallow focus selectively isolates faces/eggs but must never obscure necessary action mechanics.
- **Centered composition** is valid when a doorway/threshold must read; thirds/leading lines are aids, not compulsory positioning.

## Blocking and negative space

- Align gaze direction with room in front of the character unless deliberate claustrophobia is intended.
- Keep eyes and carried egg clear of high-contrast foliage. Avoid overlapping both characters' silhouettes when audience must differentiate them.
- During dangerous motion show the next obstacle before it is encountered; otherwise a hit feels random.
- Use value contrast before color contrast: test in grayscale and at ~20% of original size (phone approximation).
- Protect ground-plane evidence: feet, shadow, floor and obstacle should appear together when landing/catching.
- Avoid cutting characters off at knees/ankles precisely during contact moments.
- Camera labels: world-space coordinate, look-at point, scale/focal metaphor, screen direction, move type, start and end frames, parallax layers, narrative purpose, audio perspective.

## The continuity grid

Track for every transition:

| Property | Required continuity |
|---|---|
| World positions | Portal west, nest east, mother farther east, chase west unless staged turn |
| Facing and direction | Human looks/turns/runs left in chase; dinosaur follows from right |
| Axes | Maintain coherent 180° line in conversational eye-lines and pursuit; explicitly reestablish if crossing |
| Prop state | Egg in nest → falling → both hands → chest → hatching → baby held → baby on ground |
| Contact states | Foot stance and hand contacts continue across action cuts |
| Screen landmarks | Nest, portal, ground slope and branch consistent in geography |
| Camera move | Camera root separate from world coordinates and character root |
| Lighting and palette | Key-light direction, portal glow, atmospheric depth coherent |
| Sound bed | Forest continues through cuts, changes according to perspective, not random reset |

Conventions: 180° line, eyeline match, match-on-action, look room, and distinguishing adjacent shot sizes can all be deliberately broken *if the audience can reorient*. Rule-breaking must specify its dramatic purpose and verification shot.

## Camera moves by reason

| Intent | Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Discover an object | Character gaze first, gentle reframing or insert | Zoom to an unseen object before the character notices with no POV justification |
| Establish portal crossing | Locked wide/full-body with consistent ground | Shake, tight cuts, or scale changes that conceal threshold |
| Reveal threat size | Foot/ground detail followed by low medium or wide | Giant dinosaur teleport into frame |
| Convey pursuit | Lateral tracking preserving separation and obstacle visibility | Camera moving faster than the actors for no reason; frame direction reversing |
| Share realization | Hold or small push-in after perception | Constant push-in that starts before emotional event |
| Show physical contact | Follow moving object, then settle around impact | Abrupt cut just before hand/egg/ground contact |
| Deliver comedy | Hold anticipation, reveal on a deliberate beat, frame both reactions | Fast zooms before setup or premature punchline graphic |

## Motion energy and edit rhythm

- *Quiet scene* ≠ no motion: breathing, eyes, light foliage, microdelays and restrained composition give life.
- *Action scene* ≠ chaotic camera: camera may be smoother than characters to keep the geography readable. Chase intensity comes from narrowing distance, obstacle hazards, changing exertion and rhythm.
- *Emotional revelation* needs perceptual dwell time: don't cut immediately after the baby emerges. Reaction often carries the narrative change.
- *Comedy* needs setup, expected result, then surprising result. A held beat can make the portal snout reveal stronger than an arbitrary shake.
- Cut at the story change, not simply every two seconds. Continuity may require a sustained shot.

## Color and material direction

Build an eight-frame color script (one plate per scene), using Pixar's colorscript notion as inspiration, not imitating a specific film: forest warm green/cyan wonder; egg warm accents; mother menace cool shadows and compressed contrast; chase high-separation directional palette; hatch gentle warm accent; reunion softened color; follower playful cyan/green; apartment cool neutral geometry interrupted by saturated portal.

Paper-cut constraints: consistent texture size in world space; character seams are deliberate rig joints disguised under designed layered cut paper; contact shadows and light direction; soft translucent portal glow distinct from paper surface; no texture swimming as cameras move; stable shape language and consistent character model scale.

## Film grammar failure modes to catch

- Insert used as narrative shorthand where the decisive action should have been visible.
- Camera pan/zoom substitutes for actual human head-turn/footstep performance.
- A close shot erases orientation so the next cut makes the mother appear on the wrong side.
- Scale of dinosaur changes between shots with no camera or depth reason.
- Multiple high-contrast moving elements fight for attention.
- Moving camera causes character and paper textures to slide at inconsistent rates.
- A visual effects hit occurs before the physical collision or lacks deceleration/recovery.

## Review procedure

Watch 1) no sound, 2) grayscale thumbnail, 3) full size, 4) single-frame adjacent cuts, 5) camera-guide/debug overlays. The only reliable approval is actual rendered evidence plus intended/observed comparison. See `05_QA/`.
