# What makes animation good? An operational rubric

Good animation is not the maximum amount of movement. It is a **clear, intentional performance** delivered through credible physical relationships, coherent visuals and appropriate sound.

## Seven independent review dimensions (do not average away critical failures)
1. **Story clarity:** the event and its motivation can be understood without caption substitution. Check action-before/action-after stills plus silent playback.
2. **Character acting:** gaze, pose, balance and expression tell the same intention. Track perceivable thought changes.
3. **Mechanics:** supports, trajectories, contacts, inertia and deformations fit the chosen physical/stylistic world. Inspect intermediate frames and event curves.
4. **Staging and eye trace:** one priority at a time, deliberate shot size, silhouettes, eyelines, axes and reveals.
5. **Temporal rhythm:** sufficient anticipation, meaningful accents, appropriate recovery and variation rather than uniform motion.
6. **Sound narrative:** environmental identity, source/distance, transients, perspective, musical arc and useful silence. Listen blind when possible.
7. **Craft and delivery:** stable models and asset scale, clean compositing, correct safe areas, frame integrity and intelligible output.

## No-compensation rule
A great score cannot redeem an invisible catch; beautiful character sheets cannot redeem a nonfunctional rig; sharp renders cannot redeem an incoherent action. Any critical action-completion, continuity or safety failure blocks approval.

## Three observer modes
- **Silent story test:** watch at full speed, then key contact frames; report what happened and why.
- **Visual-only mechanics test:** disable decorative effects and remove camera shake; inspect stance, grip, geometry, occlusion and secondary motion.
- **Audio-only test:** listen for source, space, emotional priority, synchronization and whether any key action is implied misleadingly.

## Context changes standards
Threat may employ withholding and asymmetry; comedy may use a delayed hold and surprise snap; wonder may lengthen reveal/settle; tenderness may prioritize small gaze/contact. Their technical geometry still needs coherence. See `CONTEXT_AND_STORY.md`.
