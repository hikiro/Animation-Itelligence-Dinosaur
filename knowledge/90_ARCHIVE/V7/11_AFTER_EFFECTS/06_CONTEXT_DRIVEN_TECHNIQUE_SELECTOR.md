# Context-driven technique selection: what the tool should *mean*

A generic list of effects is not a director. Each effect must express story context. Before picking any AE feature, answer the six questions:

1. **WHO** perceives and causes the event? (knowledge and intent)
2. **WHAT** physically happens? (threshold, contact, trajectory, force)
3. **WHEN** do audience and character learn? (suspense, surprise, dramatic irony)
4. **HOW** should it feel? (fear, humor, curiosity, pain, relief)
5. **WHERE** is the observer/camera and auditory perspective? (objective, subjective, insert)
6. **WHAT PROVES** it happened? (pass/fail frames and audible cues)

## Context ↔ technique matrix

| Intent / situation | Character motion | Camera | Layers/effects/expressions | Sound | What to test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quiet curiosity | Eye lead, delayed head, soft steps | Stable medium push-in, generous negative space | Parallax cards; subtle sinusoidal plant movement | Quiet ambience with one nearby interesting cue | Viewer notices object before character acts |
| Threat approaching unseen | Character feels vibration, orients to source | Hold on reaction; keep threat offscreen | Ground/leaf vibrations via measured keyframes; shadows | Distant low-frequency stomps, environmental response | Reaction follows a plausible audible/physical stimulus |
| Panic escape | Recoil, planted push-off, coordinated gait | Tracking with preserved direction | Rig controllers, contacts, limited blur, speed graph | Footfalls, breaths, nearer source, music priority | No feet slide; distance/angle coherent |
| Comic surprise | Anticipation hold → burst → settle | Stable for anticipation, sudden reveal at payoff | Occasional held poses, overshoot, isolated pop accent | Short rhythmic punctuation, deliberately timed silence | Punchline reads muted and with audio |
| Romantic recognition | Eye contact, relaxed body, subtle settling | Two-shot/reaction close-up | Delicate secondary motion only | Intimate ambience, soften music, reaction breaths | Gaze links exact participants |
| High-energy projectile insert | Shooter reacts, object travels, receiving character anticipates/gets struck | Departure/insert/arrival shots or motivated track | Separate object, trail, matte, speed lines, time remap, event-bound accent | Launch transient, travel perspective, impact transient, short aftermath | Travel line and contact/force visible without accents |
| Heartbreaking realization | Minimal movement, small delayed inhale | Hold longer than conventional action beat | Restrained face controls, small posture collapse | Ambience shifts, music thins or stops | Emotional intent reads, not floaty inaction |

## Natural-language expansion algorithm

For each verb (e.g., “shoot”, “grab”, “exit”, “crash”, “meet”, “fall”):

1. Convert into causal beats: `stimulus, noticing, decision, anticipation, initiation, trajectory, contact, force transfer, recovery, consequence`.
2. List required actors, props, point of contact, world coordinates, possession/visibility state.
3. Plan key poses and frame intervals. If frames not specified, propose beat ratios and adjust after animatic.
4. Design camera **only for audience knowledge** and location continuity.
5. Design sound perspective and synchronization: contact-locked events vs editorial prelaps.
6. Map to layers/rigs/mattes/keyframes/expressions; avoid claiming one effect does all.
7. Define concrete visible/audible tests that reject missing action, even if composition is attractive.
8. Record alternatives when realism and stylization conflict.
9. Render, inspect, revise, log a source-backed lesson, never invent AE success.

## Three interpretations of “make the bullet impact powerful”

**Grounded drama:** Hold a wider view; show shooter recoil → quick travel cue → precise contact → character balance failure; small transient sound and restrained camera. Do not over-stylize an inherently serious moment.

**Stylized action:** Brief departure insert; directionally consistent projectile close-up and speed lines; one defined impact frame; body recoil travels through spine and weight, then a held aftermath. Controlled manga language, no aimless shake.

**Comedy:** Long setup/hold, sudden small pop, exaggerated delayed reaction, bounce/settle; subtle sound contrast. Do not use graphic injury.

All three use different camera/audio/motion treatments for the SAME causal action. The context selects the technique.
