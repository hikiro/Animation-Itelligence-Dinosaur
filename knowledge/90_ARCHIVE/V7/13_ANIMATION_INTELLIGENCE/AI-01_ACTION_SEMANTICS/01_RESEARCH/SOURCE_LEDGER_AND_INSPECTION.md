# Inspected scripts, drawn boards, and professional production sources

All observations here are *paraphrases*, linked to identifiable source sections; images/scripts themselves are not reproduced. URLs also in `02_CORPUS/SOURCE_CATALOG.json`. Audit date 2026-11-19.

## Direct readings

1. **BBC Doctor Who S10E1 shooting script (2016)** — https://downloads.bbc.co.uk/writersroom/scripts/Doctor-Who-S10-Ep1.pdf
   - PDF p.18: an apparent departure reverses after observing fear. Annotate **actor belief vs goal revision**. PDF screenshot viewed.
   - PDF p.26: a character notices another's altered demeanor, which motivates a question. This is script-level attention direction.
   - PDF p.40: machine lurch, brace against console, landing contact. Test the distinction between stimulus, body support and event sound.
   - PDF p.59: a blocked doorway forces squeezing through a restricted opening. The language does not itself specify full rig poses or camera masking.
   - **Limit:** copyrighted script; do not embed text in corpora or claim these are measured film motion.

2. **BBC Radio Drama Format (Scene Style)** — https://downloads.bbc.co.uk/writersroom/scripts/bbcradioscene.pdf
   - PDFs p.1–3 inspected, screenshots for pp.1–2. Explicitly separates dialogue, technical sound cues, transitions, microphone proximity, off-mic speech and vocal treatment.
   - **Learning:** sound adjectives need source, position, perspective and narrative role, not just a keyword sample.

3. **Bournemouth University, Once Upon a Time in Animation**, pp.13–14 — https://animationarchive.bournemouth.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/OnceUponATimeInAnimation.pdf
   - p.13: six illustrated boards progress from telescope-gazing to encounter, gesture and new framing. Screenshot directly examined.
   - p.14: three illustrated *Friends & Heroes* panels, production headers and dialogue fields. Screenshot directly examined.
   - **Learning:** drawn boards show pose/framing change and editorial grouping; animatic timing requires subsequent timed playback.

4. **Disney Animation — Story** — https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/story/
   - Direct page reading confirms story artists coordinate emotion, expression, timing, staging and framing; rough thumbnails are refined from script to storyboard.
   - **Limit:** linked images not copied into kit; don't assert action details from unseen thumbnails.

5. **Science Behind Pixar — Story and Art** — https://sciencebehindpixar.org/pipeline/story-and-art
   - The museum page describes a *Luca* sequence at the story stage, plus animation thumbnails and model packets; useful for distinction among script, board, rig plan and rendered action.
   - **Limit:** textual description inspected, not a frame-accurate audiovisual analysis of the scene.

6. **Wonder Unit — Storyboarder** — https://wonderunit.com/software/storyboarder/
   - Tool explicitly supports drawing storyboards and starting from a Fountain screenplay; relevant to reproducible text-to-board experiments.

7. **Blender Studio Spring — Open content overview** — https://studio.blender.org/blog/spring-spring-open-content-overview/
   - Production materials reportedly include storyboards, previs and final shots. Only overview directly inspected; individual shot bundles not inspected. **Future real-pair candidate, not already annotated data.**

8. **Pixar in a Box — The Art of Storytelling** — https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling
   - Public course index describes film grammar, pitch, storyboards and editorial timing. Full video lessons were not independently inspected here.

9. **Sound on Sound — Sound Design for Visual Media** — https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/sound-design-visual-media
   - Search-visible overview separates atmospheres, Foley and spot effects; full page access was blocked. Mark limited-source evidence.

10. **Adobe After Effects — Animation basics** — https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/animate-in-after-effects/animation-basics/animation-basics.html
    - Graph Editor and keyframes control motion curves; not evidence of acting quality by themselves.

## Research rule

A source can establish **what a script/board says or shows**, not that a different generated output will look good. Our authored examples stay in L2 until actually rendered and evaluated. See `03_CASE_STUDIES/SCRIPT_AND_DRAWN_BOARD_READINGS.md` and structured `INSPECTED_SOURCE_OBSERVATIONS.jsonl`.
